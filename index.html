Optimal Control Numerical Analysis Mathematical Biology

Quadrature-Induced Local Minima in Structure-Preserving Lotka-Volterra Control

Alexandre Autran

ArXiv preprint, 2026 PDF soon |

Even when a numerical model keeps populations positive, the way it counts the harvest can change the control it recommends. In a diffusive Lotka–Volterra model, I compare harvest rewards evaluated before and after each time step while keeping the same positive state scheme. The old-state rule can overcount the catch; in a pure-removal example, this changes the curvature of the objective and creates a strict local minimum that is not globally optimal. The two discrete harvest densities are

\[ H_{\mathrm{old}}^{n} = \tau q\,u^{n}v_{2}^{n}, \qquad H_{\mathrm{flux}}^{n} = \tau q\,u^{n}v_{2}^{n+1}. \]

Here \(v_2\) is the prey density, \(u\) the effort, \(q\) the catchability and \(\tau\) the time step. For the one-dimensional coupled model, I obtain mesh-uniform \(O(\tau)\) bounds on the cost, gradient and Hessian differences under the stated regularity assumptions, and derive exact discrete adjoints. Numerical tests examine the effects on computed controls; global optimality is certified only in the reduced example.

Stochastic Control Mathematical Finance Fractional Brownian Motion

Singular Tracking of Fractional Brownian Motion under Proportional Costs

Alexandre Autran

ArXiv preprint, 2026 — submitted to Stochastic Processes and their Applications PDF soon |

Tracking a fluctuating target involves a trade-off: adjusting too often is expensive, but waiting too long increases the error. I study this balance for fractional Brownian motion, using causal controls of finite variation, quadratic tracking loss and proportional adjustment costs. As the cost parameter \(\lambda\) tends to zero, the optimal value satisfies

\[ V_{\lambda}(T;a,b,\sigma) \sim \kappa_H T\, a^{\frac{1-H}{1+H}} (\lambda b)^{\frac{2H}{1+H}} \sigma^{\frac{2}{1+H}}, \qquad \lambda\downarrow0. \]

Here \(H\in(0,1)\) describes the target's regularity, \(a\) and \(b\) weight the two costs, \(\sigma\) is the target scale and \(\kappa_H>0\) is a long-run cell value. The exponent makes the role of roughness explicit. Knowing the entire past can change finite-horizon costs, but leaves the leading constant unchanged. I also extend the limit to suitable Gaussian Volterra targets and give bounds and constructive approximations for the cell value.

Probability Stochastic Analysis Mean-Field Systems

Conditional Fluctuations of Mean-Field Systems with Stable Common Noise

Alexandre Autran

ArXiv preprint, 2026 — under review at Electronic Journal of Probability PDF ↗ |

In a large interacting population, individual randomness is mixed with shocks shared by everyone. I study a smooth particle system on a torus whose common shocks have a symmetric stable law with \(0<\alpha<1\). Under a common Poisson construction, conditioning on the full source of common jumps reveals a Gaussian limit for the sampling fluctuations:

\[ \mathcal{L}\!\left( \sqrt{N}(\mu^{N}-\mu) \,\middle|\, \mathscr{G}^{0} \right) \xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}} \mathcal{K}_{\mathscr{G}^{0}}. \]

Here \(\mu^N\) is the empirical measure, \(\mu\) its conditional mean-field limit and \(\mathcal{K}_{\mathscr{G}^{0}}\) a centered Gaussian kernel depending on the common noise. For one-dimensional microscopic approximations, I identify the tail threshold at which a correction shifts the conditional mean or overtakes sampling noise. A final example shows why coupling matters: the same microscopic laws can have different path fluctuations depending on how they are linked to the limiting common signal.

Probability Stochastic Analysis Jump Diffusions

Second-Order Wasserstein Response for Lévy Laws

Alexandre Autran

ArXiv preprint, 2026 — under review at Electronic Journal of Probability PDF ↗ |

Two small changes to a jump model can look equally close at the level of their Lévy characteristics and still produce very different errors in the resulting distributions. I study this sensitivity for infinitely divisible laws with Gaussian smoothing, deriving first- and second-order responses in the Wasserstein-1 distance. In particular, a balanced redistribution of jumps can cancel the first-order effect and leave a quadratic error:

\[ W_1(\lambda_{\varepsilon},\lambda_0) = \varepsilon^2\lVert Q\rVert_{L^1} + o(\varepsilon^2), \qquad \varepsilon\to0. \]

Here \(Q\) is the second-order response of the cumulative distribution function when the first response vanishes. Under the stated moment assumptions, its norm is positive if the displacement dispersion is nonzero. The analysis gives sharp error rates for local balanced remeshing, a linear-programming approach to jump approximation, and the transition as Gaussian smoothing disappears.

Semiconductor Physics Device Modeling Applied Mathematics

Multi-Dimensional Charge Diffusion–Collection Model in Semiconductor Devices Subjected to Single Ionizing Particles

Alexandre Autran et al.

Applied Sciences, 2026 Online ↗ |

An ionizing particle crossing a semiconductor creates a cloud of charge carriers. Some spread toward a sensitive node, while others recombine before they can be collected. We develop a unified analytical description of this diffusion–collection process in one, two and three dimensions, using an idealized medium and a finite carrier lifetime:

\[ \frac{\partial n}{\partial t} = D\Delta n-\frac{n}{\tau}, \qquad Q_{\mathrm{col}}(t) = \int_0^t I_{\mathrm{col}}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s. \]

Here \(n\) is the carrier density, \(D\) the diffusion coefficient and \(\tau\) the carrier lifetime. From the point-source solution, we derive expressions for the density, diffusion current and collected charge, including a Bessel-function representation. The analysis makes the role of geometry explicit and shows that the current peaks before the carrier density in every dimension considered. A minimal Python implementation supports integration into simulation workflows and single-event sensitivity studies.
